Pitch Run of Show

Fifteen and a half minutes across fourteen slides, with a five minute live demo in the middle. Two judges are scoring the same six criteria at once, so every moment below is marked with who it is for. Nail one judge and ignore the other and the ceiling is a 3 no matter how good the build is.

Runtime 15:30 Slides 14 Demo 5:00 Presenters 3

Business owner

The executive. Listening for the customer problem, the decision, the business value. In the room this is the VP Manufacturing Operations concern: unplanned downtime.

AI spend owner

Accountable for AI cost and governance. Listening for what the AI costs, what limits are set, what the logs capture. In the room this is the Director of Manufacturing IT and Finance concern.

Section 01The problem

Opens for the business owner. The job here is that the problem is theirs and stated in their words, not a feature tour. Do not mention Databricks products in this section at all.

010:30
Predictive Maintenance for Continuous Production Both

"We are going to show you a working system, not a concept. Every number in this deck was measured on a build we finished, and in about ten minutes we will switch to it running live. We want you to leave with two things: what this is worth on your downtime line, and exactly what the AI costs to run."

Naming both takeaways in the opening tells each judge their question is on the agenda. It costs eight seconds and it sets up criterion 6.

020:45
Thirty three million a year Business owner

"Fifteen hundred hours of unplanned downtime a year across eight plants, at twenty two thousand dollars an hour. That is thirty three million, before expedited freight and SLA penalties. The interesting part is not the size of it. It is that most of it is detectable weeks in advance."

That last sentence is the hinge of the whole pitch. Land it and pause.

031:00
Why Current Monitoring Misses the Window Business owner

"You already have the sensors. The gap is not instrumentation, it is timing. A bearing defect is impulsive: early on it changes the shape of the vibration, not the amount. An RMS alarm measures the amount. So an alarm tuned not to cry wolf is blind for the first eighteen days."

The line to land: "Your monitoring is not broken. It is measuring the wrong statistic for early detection." That reframes the conversation from a procurement problem into a physics problem, and it is generous about their existing investment.

040:45
What We Measure: The Early Window Both

"Over the thirty days before a bearing failure: kurtosis up forty eight percent, crest factor up twenty seven percent, RMS up two percent. That two percent is exactly why your current alarms stay quiet."

Three numbers, one point. Do not explain what kurtosis is unless asked. If asked, it is "how spiky the signal is, versus how strong it is".

Section 02The proof

Still the business owner's section, but this is where technical credibility gets established for criterion 2. Volunteer the validation method before anyone asks for it.

050:45
Reference Implementation Both

"One hundred and fifty one instrument tags, twenty five assets, twenty one point seven million readings in a single day, and a live mass balance closing to a hundredth of a percent on a twenty five hundred tonne an hour feed. This is a mining plant, a continuous process. The architecture moves to your discrete lines unchanged."

One aside for the spend owner, eight seconds: "and note what is not in that list: no GPU cluster, and no fine tuning run." It plants the cost argument early so slide 12 is a callback rather than a surprise.

061:00
Early Detection: 95.8% vs 14.8% Both

"In the window where intervention is still possible, the model catches ninety five point eight percent of degrading readings. An RMS threshold catches fourteen point eight. We validated leave one asset out: trained on the other assets, tested on one held out completely."

Say why that matters, unprompted: "If we had split rows at random we would have leaked, because two adjacent ten minute windows are nearly identical. That is the most common way a number like this gets inflated." Volunteering the trap you avoided is the single most efficient credibility move in the deck.

Do not say "ninety five percent accurate". It is recall on degrading readings in a specific window, and a judge who knows the difference will ask.

070:45
Precision: Zero False Positives Business owner

"Fifteen vibration sensors. The model flagged four. Exactly those four failed. On the other eleven it scored zero point zero zero zero. A control room that cries wolf gets ignored inside a week, so this number matters as much as the first one."

Section 03The demo

Five minutes, and the largest single block of score on the sheet. It carries criterion 5 outright and it is the only place criterion 1 can be earned, because the three milestones have to be shown connected rather than asserted. The three beats below are the three milestones in order. Narrate them alternating personas.

  1. One governed source Milestone 1 · governed data serving the app

    Open on the flowsheet. "This layout is not drawn by hand. The app reads the plant topology from the same two tables that feed the mass balance pipeline, so these two views cannot disagree. One definition of the plant, governed in Unity Catalog, serving both."

    Point at an estimated arc. "This stream is styled differently because it is inferred, not measured. Showing an inferred number as if it were measured is how you lose a control room's trust permanently." Spend owner That detail reads as governance maturity, not UI polish.

  2. The floor acting on it Milestone 2 · the app acting on the data

    Move to the alarm strip. Acknowledge an alarm and let them see the write land. "That acknowledgement just wrote back to the operational database. This is not a dashboard you look at, it is a system of record you act in, and the acknowledgement is part of the audit trail."

    Then the asset detail view with the ISO 10816 zone bands. Business owner "This is the screen a maintenance planner opens. The bands are the international standard your engineers already work to, so nobody has to learn our scale."

  3. AI you can audit Milestone 3 · the AI inside it governed

    Ask the assistant a real question, ideally "what needs maintenance this week and why". Business owner "The answer cites the assets and the reason. A planner can act on that without opening a notebook."

    Then hand over to the spend owner and stay on this beat. Spend owner Open the AI Gateway view on the endpoint. "Every call through this endpoint is rate limited at two hundred and forty a minute, usage tracked, and logged to a table in your own catalog tagged by plant and asset. A runaway client cannot become a runaway invoice, and if you ever need to answer what the AI told someone in March, the payload is there."

    Do not open the gateway view cold. See pre-flight: usage data arrives on a delay, and an empty panel here converts your strongest governance moment into a visible fault.

Section 04The business case

090:45
Unplanned Downtime Reduction Business owner

"The question we keep getting from operations is this one: did a plant manager make a different call this shift? On the reference build, yes. Three bearing defects and a screen failure, each with more than eighteen days of notice. Maintenance was scheduled instead of scrambled."

The slide no longer names anyone, so attribute it in the room instead: "this is the question your VP of Manufacturing Operations asked us". Speaking the name is warm; printing it on a slide is not.

101:00
The Business Case Business owner

"Thirty three million today. If ten percent of those hours convert from unplanned to planned, that is three point three million a year."

Then immediately give the number back: "We are not claiming ten percent. We are claiming eighteen days of notice. Ten percent is what that notice is worth if your maintenance function acts on it, and your asset mix decides the real figure. That is what the pilot is for." Executives discount numbers they sense are being oversold, so bounding it yourself makes the rest of the deck more credible, not less.

Section 05AI spend

The spend owner's signature moment and the one criterion where a generic answer caps you at a 3. The value story has to be about Volta specifically, not about serverless in general.

110:30
AI Spend at Scale Spend owner

"The other question we were asked: if we roll this to eight plants, what does it cost? Cost scales with plants and data, not with experiments. Adding a tenth data scientist costs almost nothing here."

121:30
What the AI Actually Costs Spend owner

"The cheapest decision we made on this build was choosing not to use a language model for the detection job. Detection is a gradient boosted model on twelve numeric features. It scores twenty one million readings a day and it consumes zero tokens, because there is no language in the problem. Vibration is a number."

"We do use a language model, in exactly one place: answering a person's question on shift. That means your token spend tracks how often somebody asks something, not how much your plant measures. Those two cost curves are completely different, and picking the wrong one is how AI programmes get expensive."

Then the three controls, briefly: a rate limit of two hundred and forty calls a minute enforced at the gateway, usage tracked per plant and per asset, and one hundred percent of inferences written to a table in their catalog. "Governance is not a policy document here, it is configuration on the endpoint."

Close on their number, not ours: "So the honest answer to what this costs at eight plants is that the detection layer scales with sensors and stays off tokens entirely, and we size the rest against your data volume in the pilot."

This is the slide where a generic answer costs you the criterion. Do not describe serverless in the abstract. Every sentence should be about a crushing plant with twenty one million readings a day.

Section 07Under questioning

Criterion 2 is scored on whether the answer is adapted to whoever asked. Same fact, different framing. Whoever built a layer should answer for it: the builder answering is what reads as expertise.

BIZHow do we know this works on our equipment?

"You do not yet, and neither do we. What we know is that it works on equipment it had never seen, because that is how we validated it: held one asset out entirely and tested on that. The pilot answers it properly on twelve months of your own telemetry."

BIZWhat if it misses a failure?

"It will. We are not proposing you switch off your inspections. We are proposing you get eighteen days of notice on the failures it does catch, and on the reference build that was four out of four bearing and screen failures. This is added warning, not replaced process."

BIZMy maintenance team is already at capacity.

"That is the argument for it rather than against it. Planned work costs less labour than an emergency: no overtime call out, no expedited freight, parts already on site. This converts work you are already doing into a cheaper form of the same work."

SPENDWhat is the actual run rate?

"Detection carries no token cost at any volume, and the compute scales to zero between runs, so there is no idle baseline. The language layer is bounded by how many questions people ask on shift. We will not quote you a monthly figure before we have sized your data volume, and you should not trust anyone who does."

SPENDWhat stops this becoming a runaway bill?

"A rate limit on the endpoint, configured today at two hundred and forty calls a minute. A misbehaving client gets throttled, not billed. The limit is configuration you control, and it is enforced at the gateway rather than trusted to application code."

SPENDWhere do the logs go, and who can read them?

"Every inference is written to a table in your own Unity Catalog, tagged with the plant and the asset it was about. Access is governed by the same catalog permissions as the rest of your data. Nothing about the AI sits outside the governance you already run."

SPENDWhy not just use an LLM for all of it?

"Because it is the wrong instrument and a far more expensive one. Vibration is a number, not language, so a language model would cost orders of magnitude more to get a worse answer, and it would give you a different answer on the same input twice. You cannot put a non deterministic number in a maintenance audit trail. We use language where the task is language, and nowhere else."

TECHWhat features does the model use at serving time?

Answer precisely, because it is checkable: the deployed endpoint takes twelve features, including three vibration trend features (one hour delta, twenty four hour delta and twenty four hour slope). Those come from the feature pipeline, not from a raw reading, which is why scoring runs against computed features rather than the live tag stream.

TECHHow do you handle model drift?

"Today, honestly: retraining is a job you run, not an automatic loop. The inference table gives you the input distribution over time, which is what you would monitor drift against. We would rather tell you that than claim a closed loop we have not built."

TECHIs any of this real time, or is it batch?

The live tiles read the operational database directly, which is why that layer exists. The analytics refresh runs on a five minute schedule. Say that plainly: the flowsheet is minutes fresh, the tag values are current. Claiming sub second end to end would not survive a follow up question.

Section 08Pre-flight

Criterion 5 penalises any visible fault, including running out of time. Work through these before you present, in order.

  • Drive traffic through the endpoint at least an hour before you present. Usage tracking and inference logging both arrive on a delay. Forty real calls were made during setup and system.serving.endpoint_usage and pdm_gateway_payload were still empty minutes later. If you open the gateway view cold it will be blank, which turns your best governance moment into a visible fault.
  • Have the fallback ready and know when to reach for it. The stub_* tables still exist and the app can be pointed at them with a one line config change. If a live panel fails, switch and say so plainly. A presenter who says "that is the live feed misbehaving, here is the same view on captured data" reads as competent; silence while clicking does not.
  • Confirm the analytics refresh is unpaused. The five minute schedule is what makes the flowsheet move. If it is paused the demo looks frozen and every freshness claim in section 03 is wrong.
  • Decide who narrates each demo beat and rehearse the two handovers. Whoever built a layer answers for it. Fumbled handovers are the most common way a strong demo drops to a 3.
  • Time the demo once, with a stopwatch. Five minutes is short. Overrunning it costs criterion 5 directly and eats the AI spend section, which is where a whole criterion lives.
  • Agree who takes which question. Business questions to whoever opened, spend and governance questions to whoever narrated beat three, model questions to whoever trained it.
  • Open the app, the gateway view and the deck in separate tabs, in order. No searching for a URL in front of the judges.
